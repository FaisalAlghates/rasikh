- Engineering office: the assistant now designs 6–12 options (8 by default) for a brief. It reads more of the client's words (bathroom count, guest room, study, prayer room, gym, store, prep kitchen, target built-up area, budget, plot size; "دورات مياه" is no longer read as floors), searches about 1,700 layout variants (stair side, entrance, front or back yard, side passage, dining and master-suite position, band split, size, mirroring), keeps only options that hold every requested room, pass every zoning rule and every automatic code rule, give every room a proper door and clear the duct/beam check, then picks the most different ones (a mirror image does not count). Each option shows why it fits the brief, its checks in plain words (code rules read "passed" only after an office engineer verifies their values; fire, energy, structure, electrical and plumbing always go to the engineer) and a 17-item bill of quantities in 7 sections with a cost range, cost per m², CSV download and a comparison table with the client's budget. Layout fixes: every habitable room and bathroom reaches both the corridor and an outside wall, en-suites stay together, deep bathrooms get a closet, and the floor split respects the room widths.
- Engineering office, the site: every option now draws the client's parking bays (perpendicular or parallel to the street in the front yard, tandem down a side passage), a pool when asked for (back yard first, 1.2 m clear around it) and a walkway from the street to each entrance; a separate guest entrance opens from the facade straight into the men's majlis. - Municipal requirements: the Ministry of Municipalities and Housing's unified "اشتراطات إنشاء المباني السكنية" (ministerial decision 1/4500943139, 1446 AH / 2024), binding on every Amanah, are built in for villas and apartment buildings with their clauses (setbacks from the street width: a fifth, at least 3 m, 6 m on streets of 30 m or more; 1.5 m to neighbours; 75 % per floor; two floors and a roof annex of 70 % of the floor below; parking by plot area). The office applies them to the plot in one step; the height, which each area's approved local plan sets, is flagged for the office to confirm. The Rasikh team keeps each Amanah's own requirements with their source in /admin (rules: team-only writes, everyone signed in reads). The source document shows on the checks and the option cards.
- Roof annex: drawn when the brief asks for it and the requirements allow one, over the core, no larger than the allowed share and 1.5 m off facades on streets (Z-ANNEX), with a roof terrace in front; not counted as a floor.
- New automatic code checks (starting values, "not checked" until an office engineer verifies them): inner court width (C-COURT), window area for daylight (C-GLAZING), room for the stair's steps and landings (C-STAIR-RUN).
- Design library the engine learns from: after a release the client may agree to share the design without their data; with that consent the office lead adds it (the pattern only, or with the plan), credited to the office (designLibrary/{project}, rules-enforced; the client can withdraw at any time). When designing, the engine re-lays the patterns of similar library designs and of the office's own released projects on the new plot, checks them like every option, prefers an inspired design within a type when it ranks close to the best, nudges the ranking toward what offices choose, and credits the source office on the option. Offices browse the library in their workspace. Nothing is collected from outside Rasikh: other offices' drawings are their work and their clients' property; openly licensed plan datasets were reviewed (Swiss Dwellings is CC BY 4.0 but holds Swiss apartments only; CubiCasa5K is non-commercial).
- What may be built where: a catalogue of 88 property types (villas, palaces, rest houses, chalets, farms, apartments, hotels, studios, offices, shops, showrooms, halls, mosques, restaurants, warehouses, workshops, hospitals, schools, airports ...) with their SBC occupancy, the land uses each may stand on (residential, commercial, agricultural, industrial, investment, public facilities) and the engine family that designs it. The engine now designs hall buildings (wedding and event halls, mosques, restaurants, gyms, warehouses, workshops, showrooms, farm buildings) and unit buildings by type (hotel rooms, studios, offices, clinics, shops); specialist buildings (hospitals, airports, factories, malls ...) get land-use and requirement checks only. Property status labels (for sale, off plan, heritage, furnished ...) are read from the brief. Windows are sized for daylight; larger buildings may park beyond the yards (basement or structure).
